What's up,

I just made a finding that a lot of our customers need their results in a specific format for their thesis or report.

Like... actual statistical test output (test statistic, degrees of freedom, p-value) that they can cite directly in their results chapter. 

What I'd like us to add: together with the existing visualizations you're currently working on, generate a statistical results summary using standard inferential tests. 

In order of priority:

1. Chi-square test of independence (categorical × categorical) → χ², df, p-value, Cramér's V
2. Independent samples t-test (2 groups, continuous outcome) → t-statistic, df, p-value
3. One-way ANOVA (3+ groups, continuous outcome) → F-statistic, df, p-value
4. Pearson/Spearman correlation (two continuous/ordinal variables) → r or rho, p-value
5. Cronbach's alpha (reliability of multi-item scales)
6. Nice-to-have: simple linear regression → coefficients, R², p-value

Output requirements:
• Present results in APA format (e.g. "χ²(2) = 8.32, p = .016") so they're directly citable

• Add a one-line plain-language interpretation per test ("There is a statistically significant association between X and Y")

• Include these in the exportable report alongside the existing charts

From what I know, the trickiest part is probably the test-selection logic, which is auto-detecting which test applies based on the variable types being compared (categorical vs continuous, number of groups, etc.). 

These are standard stats library functions (scipy.stats or equivalent), let me know your thoughts on this pls... Scope and possible delivery date.
