1. The Graph Types (From Basic to Advanced)
Here is the complete breakdown of every visualization graph type available and exactly what it is best used for:

Level 1: Basic (Single Question Distribution) These graphs look at exactly one question and count how many times each answer was picked.

bar (Bar Chart): The gold standard. Best for multiple-choice questions or 5-point scales. It clearly shows the frequency of each option side-by-side.
pie (Pie Chart): Best for questions with only a few distinct choices (like Yes/No, or Gender) to visually represent the "share" of the total responses.
line / area (Line & Area Charts): Best for ordered scales (like a rating from 1 to 10) to visually see if the trend is skewing positive or negative.
treemap: A beautiful alternative to a pie chart. It displays choices as nested colored rectangles, where the size of the rectangle represents how popular the answer was.
Level 2: Intermediate (Cross-Tabulation) These graphs compare two questions against each other (e.g., comparing "Course Satisfaction" across different "Genders" or "Departments").

stacked_bar & clustered_bar: Takes a standard bar chart and splits each bar by a demographic. (e.g., You can see exactly how many Males vs. Females voted "Strongly Agree").
heatmap: A colored matrix/grid that shows the intensity of responses between two variables. Darker colors represent heavy overlaps.
scatter (Scatter Plot): Plots individual responses as dots on an X/Y axis. Excellent for spotting correlation between two numerical questions (e.g., Age vs. Income).
Level 3: Statistical Analysis

box_plot (Box & Whisker): Strictly for numerical data. It automatically calculates the minimum, average, median, and maximum values, and highlights any weird "outliers" (people who voted drastically differently than everyone else).
Level 4: Advanced Machine Learning

dendrogram (Hierarchical Clustering): Powered by the Python microservice. It uses the Ward clustering algorithm to build a "family tree" of your survey data.
network: Visualizes how closely related certain answers or respondents are, drawing lines (links) between connected nodes.
